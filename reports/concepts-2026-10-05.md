### #10 · Last Bus Home: Night Route Horror  —  horror / Co-op PvE
*Drive a haunted night bus with your friends and decide who to let aboard, because some passengers are not people.*

**Pitch.** Your crew runs one night bus down a foggy road with seven stops. At each stop a passenger waits at the door. Real passengers carry a ticket stamped with that stop, and the Things wear a ticket that is blank or slightly wrong. Let one in and it slowly kills the bus lights. Turn away a real one and you lose their fare. The team must agree on every door, together, in a few seconds.

**The gap.** Rounds are 85% of hits vs 61% of other games (lift 1.24). Can-lose-progress is 77% vs 56% (lift 2.41). Help-style social play is 54% vs 33%. No hit in the leader list is built around a shared judgement call at a door. The 11 entrants in 90 days skew to animal-care 'anomaly' jobs, so a vehicle-and-passenger setting is open.

**Core loop.** Each round is one route of 7 stops, about 9 minutes. Roles rotate every stop: Driver (steers and works the headlights), Conductor (checks tickets with a punch tool) and Lookouts (watch windows and mirrors for movement in the aisle). At each stop the crew votes by pulling the door lever: open, or drive past. Real passengers add fares to the shared till and can fix a bus system, such as the heater or a flickering light. A Thing hides in the aisle, so lookouts have to spot who is not sitting like the others, and the conductor punches or ejects it. Reaching the depot banks the till. If the lights die or the bus is wrecked, the round's till is lost.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-10s: spawn in the bus seats as the engine starts, and a big ticket-stamp prompt teaches the conductor tool. 10-30s: the first stop is a guaranteed real passenger, who pays a fare with a loud coin sound. 30-45s: the second passenger has a visibly blank ticket, and the tutorial hint tells you to stamp it. 45-60s: the Thing is exposed and you eject it for a small burst of coins and a first cosmetic token.

**Features**
- Seven-stop route with rotating Driver, Conductor and Lookout roles (judgement)
- Ticket-stamp check where the tells get subtler on later stops (judgement)
- Shared fare till that is lost if the bus is wrecked, which gives the can-lose-progress stakes (measured: lift 2.41)
- Downed players are dragged to the back seat and revived by the conductor's thermos (measured: help asymmetry in 54% of hits)
- Round-based format, about 9 minutes (measured: rounds in 85% of hits)
- Bus skins, driver hats and ticket-punch shapes as cosmetic unlocks (measured: cosmetic_only lift 2.24, weak evidence)
- A new route every week with its own road hazards: flooded, forest, mountain pass (judgement)
- Descriptive title, no codes in the description, and a pass line with no boosts (measured)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: First fare coins and a stamped-ticket token
- First session: Complete one route and unlock a bus paint colour and a driver hat
- First week: Finish all 3 launch routes and earn a route-specific bus skin
- Progression: Cosmetic collection per route. There are no stat upgrades, so skill is what carries a run.
- Retention hook: Day 2: the flooded route unlocks after the first completed run
- Retention hook: Day 8: a new weekly route with a unique bus skin that stays earnable afterwards

**Social mechanic.** Door votes and rotating roles force talking. The conductor can only see tickets and the lookouts can only see the aisle, so the crew has to relay what each of them sees. Reviving a downed friend is a shared, visible rescue.

**Monetization.** Night Driver Cosmetic Pack (100 R$: 3 bus paint jobs and a horn sound), VIP Depot Lounge (175 R$: Private-server lobby, VIP chat tag and a conductor hat), Gilded Bus Skin (249 R$: Animated skin for the bus and headlight colours). First prompt at minute 9.5. No pass touches difficulty or fares. The first prompt appears only after the first completed route (design judgement). Prices follow hit medians: min 100, median about 174.

**Content cadence.** A new weekly route with a new Thing type, plus a monthly cosmetic set. Older routes stay playable, with no limited-time lockouts.

**Versus the leaders.** DOORS and Evade are about fleeing or room-by-room puzzles, and 99 Nights in the Forest is open survival. Here you stay in one moving vehicle and the horror is a social deduction call at each door. It is a different theme and hook from the animal-care anomaly entrants (Animal Hospital, Animal Daycare, 7 Days Cat-Sitting).

### #11 · Scarecrow Field: Harvest Until Dawn  —  horror / Co-op PvE
*Scarecrows only move when nobody on your team is looking at them, so your crew has to spread out and keep eyes on every one.*

**Pitch.** Your crew has seven minutes to haul pumpkins from a moonlit field to the cart before the dawn bell. Nine scarecrows stand among the rows, and each one creeps forward whenever no teammate has it in view. If you get caught you become a scarecrow yourself, frozen in place. You can still watch, and your gaze keeps the others still until a teammate cuts you down.

**The gap.** Rounds are 85% of hits. Can-lose-progress is 77% of hits vs 56% of others (lift 2.41). The 'Escape' sub-genre has a moderate lift of 8.3 in hits, but only moderate evidence. This is a shared-vision survival round, not an escape room or a story. Half-life is about 1 day (n=7), so replayable rounds matter more than a campaign.

**Core loop.** Players spawn at a farmhouse porch and run into the field with sacks. Each player has a visible vision cone. Any scarecrow inside a teammate's cone freezes, and any outside every cone creeps closer. You pull pumpkins, carry them to the cart (heavier loads slow you down) and decide when to spread out. When a scarecrow touches you, you are turned to straw. You stand frozen but can still turn your head, so you keep watching and keep scarecrows still. A teammate rescues you by cutting you down with a sickle. The round ends at dawn with a pumpkin quota check. If the whole crew is turned to straw, the hauled pumpkins are lost.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-10s: the bell rings and the camera sweeps over the field and its scarecrows. 10-25s: you grab your first pumpkin and see a scarecrow freeze while you look at it, then creep when you turn away. 25-45s: you drop the pumpkin in the cart for a coin chime. 45-60s: a scripted scarecrow reaches a teammate, who becomes straw, and you cut them free for your first rescue star.

**Features**
- Vision-cone freeze mechanic, so the whole team's attention is the core resource (judgement)
- Turned-to-straw state where you can still look but cannot move, so you still help and are never just waiting (measured: help asymmetry in 54% of hits)
- 7-minute round ending at the dawn bell (measured: rounds lift 1.24)
- Haul lost if the entire crew is turned to straw (measured: can-lose-progress lift 2.41)
- Heavy pumpkin loads slow you down, forcing risky choices (judgement)
- Four field layouts: cornfield, orchard, hedge maze, flooded paddy (judgement)
- Descriptive title, no codes, and a pass line with no boosts (measured)
- Large scarecrow faces on the icon (measured: face on icon in 69% of hits)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: First pumpkin delivered and a coin chime
- First session: First dawn survived, with a lantern skin
- First week: Clear all four fields and earn a straw-hat set
- Progression: No persistent power. Earned titles such as 'Dawn Keeper' are shown by name, so mastery is the reward.
- Retention hook: Day 2: the orchard layout unlocks after one cleared dawn
- Retention hook: Day 8: the hedge maze with a new faster scarecrow type

**Social mechanic.** Because everyone depends on being watched, a teammate who wanders off alone makes the whole crew nervous. Callouts like 'I've got the north one!' become the core talk. The rescue sickle gives one clear way to help.

**Monetization.** Lantern Collection (100 R$: 3 cosmetic lantern designs and a trail effect), Farmhand Outfit (149 R$: 2 outfits and a scarecrow-costume emote), VIP Barn (199 R$: VIP chat tag, private-server option and a name colour). First prompt at minute 8.0. No boosts, because 54% of hits have none and the survival feel depends on fairness. The first prompt appears after the first round ends (design judgement).

**Content cadence.** One new field layout or scarecrow type every two weeks, with a seasonal skin set each month.

**Versus the leaders.** 99 Nights in the Forest is long open survival, Dandy's World is character-based co-op, and DOORS is room-by-room. Scarecrow Field is a single fixed-length round where the horror comes from where the team looks. It avoids the animal-care anomaly trend entirely.

**Scope.** ~4 weeks · 1 dev, with free or licensed asset packs for the field and a simple scarecrow rig · biggest risk: The creep-when-unseen logic must run reliably across devices with different fields of view, or it feels unfair.

### #12 · Haunted Puppet Theater: Hit Your Cues  —  horror / Co-op PvE
*Four stagehands must do the right cues on a haunted stage while a silent puppet audience watches, and anyone who misses becomes a puppet.*

**Pitch.** Each show has six short acts. A glowing cue card appears over the stage, such as 'drop the moon prop', 'stand on the star mark' or 'pull the red curtain rope'. Everyone sees the same cue card, but the actions are spread across the stage and backstage, so the team must split the work and call out who is doing what. Puppet audience members turn their heads toward whoever is late. Too many misses wake the Director, who hunts the crew between acts.

**The gap.** Rounds are 85% of hits and can-lose-progress is 77%. Hits with no social asymmetry are 46%, so a fully symmetrical crew is also a proven pattern, though it was less common among the others (64% of non-hits). Entrants like Monochrome and Threshold show new horror formats can reach 15k-20k peaks within weeks. Nothing in the leaders or the recent entrant list uses a performance or timing loop.

**Core loop.** A show is six acts of about 75 seconds each, plus a short intermission, about 8 minutes in all. In each act, cue cards appear in sequence and every player sees them all. Each cue has a location, such as a rope, a prop crate, a spotlight mark or a trapdoor, so the crew has to decide who goes where. A missed or wrong cue fills the Audience Mood bar, and the front-row puppets creep closer to the stage. At intermission, if the bar is high, the Director puppet hunts the crew in the dark wings, and players hide in prop trunks. Finishing the final curtain banks the show's ticket stubs, which are lost if all four players are turned into puppets.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-10s: lights rise on a quiet theater and the first cue card says 'Pull the red rope'. 10-25s: you pull it and the curtain opens with a satisfying creak and a cheer from the puppets. 25-45s: two cues appear at once on opposite sides of the stage, which teaches splitting. 45-60s: you hit both and get your first ticket stub and a spotlight flash.

**Features**
- Six-act show with cue cards everyone can see (judgement)
- Cues placed on both sides of the stage and backstage so the crew must split and call out (judgement)
- Audience Mood bar plus puppets that creep when cues are missed (judgement)
- Director hunt at intermission with hide-in-trunk mechanics (judgement)
- Ticket stubs lost if the whole crew is turned into puppets (measured: can-lose-progress lift 2.41)
- 8-minute rounds (measured: rounds lift 1.24)
- Three launch shows with different stages and cues (judgement)
- Descriptive title with no codes and no boost passes (measured)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: First cue hit with a curtain-open cheer and a ticket stub
- First session: Complete one full show and earn the 'Stagehand' title
- First week: Finish all 3 shows with no cue missed in one act, for a golden spotlight effect
- Progression: None. New shows are the content and skill is what carries you. Earned titles show on your nametag.
- Retention hook: Day 2: the second show unlocks after the first full show
- Retention hook: Day 8: a new show with a harder cue style and a new Director puppet

**Social mechanic.** Everyone sees the same cards, so there is no information gap. The pressure is purely coordination: who goes left, who covers the backstage rope. Friends can revive a downed player by pulling them out of the puppet row between acts.

**Monetization.** Stage Props Cosmetic Set (100 R$: 3 prop skins and a spotlight colour), Playbill Pack (175 R$: Extra stagehand outfits and nametag frames), Director's Chair (299 R$: Private show hosting and a cosmetic Director puppet skin). First prompt at minute 9.0. No VIP perk and no boosts, so this concept tests the less common pass line. The first prompt appears after the first completed show (design judgement).

**Content cadence.** A new show every two to three weeks, plus small cue-type updates in between.

**Versus the leaders.** DOORS, Dandy's World and 99 Nights in the Forest are about exploring, fleeing or surviving. This is an act-by-act coordination game where the fear comes from missing a cue. It also differs from the already-proposed Split Sight Horror Escape, where each player sees a different version of the hotel; here everyone sees the same cards.

**Declared trait profile** (what the scoring agent checks): core_loop=horror, play_mode=['coop', 'pve'], session_shape=rounds, progression_type=['none'], social_asymmetry=none, can_lose_progress=True, has_base_defense=False, offline_accrual=False, has_timers_or_fomo=False, borrows_meme_or_ip=none, title_pattern=descriptive, icon_has_face=True, icon_has_text=False, desc_has_codes=False, pass_has_boost=False, pass_has_vip=False, server_size=6, pass_count=3, pass_min_price=100, pass_median_price=175, ttfr_target_seconds=20, first_purchase_prompt_min=9.0, round_length_s=480

**Scope.** ~4 weeks · 1 dev, with 1 part-time artist for the stage and puppet models · biggest risk: Cue pacing is hard to tune: too fast and it feels random, too slow and it stops being scary.

### #13 · Hire a Ghost  —  rng / PvE
*Roll for a ghost to haunt your hotel rooms, and the spookier the ghost, the more coins it scares out of your guests.*

**Pitch.** You run a tiny haunted hotel with empty rooms. Tap Roll to hire a random ghost, from a Dusty Sheet to a Phantom Chandelier, and put it in a room. Each ghost scares guests and earns coins, even while you're away. Spend the coins on more rooms and better rolls, and chase rare ghosts to fill your Ghost Registry.

**The gap.** The measured leaders are dice, cards, luck blocks, ants, fishing and swords. None is a spooky-tenant idea. Tycoon framing is carried by 75% of this archetype's hits (lift 1.86 vs controls) but only 42% of its other games. Entrants are slowing (4 in the last 90 days vs 9 in the prior 90), so a fresh theme with a Tycoon skin has room (room score 1.0, but sufficiency only 0.4).

**Core loop.** Tap Roll to hire a ghost, rarity weighted, and place it in a room slot. Each ghost has a scare rating that sets coins per second. Coins buy Luck Candles (better rarity odds), more room slots and faster rolls. Duplicate ghosts can be merged into a higher star level, which gives a numbers-go-up path alongside the rarity chase. Every few minutes a Guest Wave arrives and your ghosts scare it automatically, giving a short payoff burst. Offline, ghosts keep haunting and coins pile up in a Lobby Safe that you collect on return.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-5s: spawn in the lobby, a glowing Roll button is already pulsing. 5-10s: first roll is a guaranteed Common ghost with a cute face, and it drops into room 1 and starts earning. 10-30s: coin counter ticks, a second roll is affordable, a guest NPC runs screaming. 30-45s: a third roll lands an Uncommon with a bigger scare number and a light show. 45-60s: a Guest Wave arrives, the ghosts react, and a teaser shows the first locked Legendary silhouette in the Registry.

**Features**
- One-tap Roll button with a visible rarity ladder and odds shown on screen (judgement)
- Ghost Registry collection book with silhouettes for undiscovered ghosts (judgement)
- 6-8 room slots at start, expandable to about 40, each with a different haunted look (judgement)
- Merge three duplicates into a starred ghost for a multiplier (judgement)
- Offline accrual into a Lobby Safe, collected on return (measured: 50% of hits, weak evidence, lift 1.72)
- No progress loss: ghosts can never be destroyed or stolen (measured: 100% of hits are can_lose_progress=false)
- No PvP or base attack: pure PvE (measured: 100% of hits are pve, no base defense)
- Persistent session with no rounds (measured: 75% of hits are persistent)
- Description has no codes, only a plain explanation of the hook (measured: 100% of hits, lift 1.16)

**Rewards and retention**

- First week: Fill the Registry's first page (about 25 ghosts) and unlock the Attic floor with a better rarity pool.
- Progression: More rooms, then floors (Lobby, Attic, Cellar, Crypt Suite). Each floor has a new ghost pool and a higher coin multiplier. Merging and Luck Candles are the numbers-go-up layer.
- Retention hook: Day 2: a Lobby Safe full of offline coins, plus a daily free Golden Roll that is never lost if you miss it.
- Retention hook: Day 8: the Crypt Suite floor unlocks, with Mythic ghost silhouettes that need merged 3-star ghosts to summon.

**Social mechanic.** No take or steal mechanic. Players see each other's hotels in the same 7-player server through a nameplate showing their rarest ghost, and a 'Boo!' emote gives both players a small coin bonus. It is a purely friendly social layer.

**Monetization.** Lucky Candle (99 R$: +10% rarity odds, permanent), 2x Scare Coins (249 R$: Doubles coin earnings (boost pass)), Auto Roll (349 R$: Rolls automatically), VIP Haunt (499 R$: VIP room skin, VIP nameplate, +25% offline cap), Extra Floor Slots (149 R$: +6 room slots), Golden Roll Pack (799 R$: Extra daily Golden Rolls and a guaranteed Epic). The price ladder follows the hits' pass medians (min 99, median 299, 6 passes).

**Content cadence.** Weekly: 2-3 new ghosts and one seasonal room skin. Every 3 weeks: a new floor with a new ghost pool. A Halloween floor and ghost pool is built in for the current season (judgement).

Here the rolled item is a worker that fills a visible, decorated hotel and earns money, so the base grows visibly while you roll. The ghosts have faces and personalities, and the Registry gives collection a purpose beyond the number.

### #15 · Catch a Comet  —  rng / PvE
