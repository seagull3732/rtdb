### #19 · Mimic Check: Which One Is the Real Me?  —  horror / Co-op PvE
*A monster copies one of your teammates exactly, and the only way to tell the fake is that real players know the squad's secret emote code while the copy just echoes whatever it saw a beat late.*

**Pitch.** Five friends are locked in a creaky boarding house at night and must collect four fuses to open the front door. A Mimic wears the exact avatar and name of one squad member and tries to stay close, then strikes. Each round the squad gets a fresh three-emote code and has to challenge suspects by emoting at them and watching who answers correctly. It is paranoia and teamwork with no PvP, because the fake is always the game's AI.

**The gap.** Hits in this archetype are round-based (79% of hits, lift 1.22) and 71% let players lose progress (lift 2.05). The leaders (99 Nights in the Forest, Dandy's World, DOORS, Sarab) are about surviving a place or a monster, not about doubting your own teammates. Nothing in the entrant list builds the scare out of who to trust. 4 of 59 games are breakouts, so a distinct hook has room (16 entrants in 90 days vs 7 before).

**Core loop.** Each round the squad spawns in the lobby hall and is shown a three-emote code card. They split up to search rooms for four fuses, which are carried items and can be dropped when downed. Once the Mimic is active (about 90 seconds in), anyone can challenge a teammate by emoting at them. Real players answer with the code and fakes echo the last emote they saw, one beat late. The code rotates every 2 minutes, so old knowledge goes stale. Carrying fuses to the fuse box and opening the front door ends the round. Squads that bank fuses unlock cosmetic mask and room-trinket drops.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-10s: spawn in the hall with a creepy-but-quick tutorial card showing the emote code. 10-25s: walk to the first room, find a fuse, and see a teammate wave oddly. 25-45s: a scripted Mimic echo teaches the late-copy tell. 45-60s: the first challenge succeeds, the first Mimic is revealed and chased off, and the squad earns the first mask fragment.

**Features**
- Three-emote secret code card with a rotating code every 2 minutes (judgement)
- Mimic AI that copies avatar, name tag and walk cycle but echoes emotes one beat late (judgement)
- 5-player squad instances with rounds of about 10 minutes, matching round-based hits (measured: 79% of hits are rounds)
- Downed players drop carried fuses and can be revived by a teammate, a cooperative 'help' loop (measured: 50% of hits use help asymmetry)
- Lose carried fuses and the round bonus when the squad wipes (measured: can_lose_progress lift 2.05)
- Hub lobby of 25 players (measured: median server_size of hits is 25) that groups into 5-player squads
- 3 boarding-house layouts and 2 Mimic behaviours at launch, so the first week is not repeating (judgement)
- Cosmetic mask collection, no stat gating (judgement)
- Face-forward icon: two identical faces, one subtly wrong (measured: 71% of hits have a face)
- Quick-challenge wheel button for phone players, so emote checks take one tap (judgement)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: First fuse picked up and the first Mimic echo spotted, with a mask fragment awarded.
- First session: Win or lose 3 rounds, with 1 mask completed and a 'Sharp Eyes' title.
- First week: Collect 3 full mask sets and a squad badge for 5 Mimics correctly caught in a row.
- Progression: Cosmetic-only: masks, room trinkets and emote variants unlocked by Mimics caught and rounds won.
- Retention hook: Day 2: a new emote code pack makes yesterday's tells less reliable.
- Retention hook: Day 8: a weekly rotating house layout with a harder Mimic variant that echoes faster.

**Social mechanic.** Players challenge each other and revive each other. The squad has to share suspicion out loud, and the fun comes from accusing a friend by mistake.

**Monetization.** Extra Emote Pack (99 R$: Cosmetic emote variants, no code advantage), Mask Collection (149 R$: Exclusive mask set and skin), Private Squad Room (199 R$: VIP private lobby for friend groups). First prompt at minute 9.0. Pass min price 99 matches hits' median (99), and 3 passes matches the hits' median. VIP is included because 50% of hits have it. No boosts, as the code and fuse mechanics should stay fair (hits are only 29% boost).

**Content cadence.** Every 2 weeks: a new house layout or a new Mimic variant. Monthly: a mask set and a holiday-themed code card.

**Versus the leaders.** DOORS and Dandy's World are about reacting to monsters, and 99 Nights in the Forest is about long-term survival. Here the monster is a copy of a friend and the tension is verifying identity. The verification test is social, not a puzzle or a hiding spot, and none of the already-proposed concepts use it.

**Declared trait profile** (what the scoring agent checks): core_loop=horror, play_mode=['coop', 'pve'], session_shape=rounds, progression_type=['cosmetic_only'], social_asymmetry=help, can_lose_progress=True, has_base_defense=False, offline_accrual=False, has_timers_or_fomo=False, borrows_meme_or_ip=none, title_pattern=descriptive, icon_has_face=True, icon_has_text=True, desc_has_codes=False, pass_has_boost=False, pass_has_vip=True, server_size=25, pass_count=3, pass_min_price=99, pass_median_price=149, ttfr_target_seconds=30, first_purchase_prompt_min=9.0, round_length_s=600

**Scope.** ~5 weeks · 1 scripter and 1 builder/modeller · biggest risk: Mimic behaviour must feel fair on mobile, and players may just spam challenges. Add a cooldown and a cost to challenging.

### #20 · Floor Rules: One Lie Per Elevator Ride  —  horror / Co-op PvE
*Every floor posts five safety rules on the elevator wall and exactly one is a lie, so your squad has to work out which before the doors open.*

**Pitch.** Four co-workers ride a rusty service elevator down through eight floors of an abandoned department store. Each floor shows a torn notice of five rules like 'never turn on the lights'. One rule is a trap planted by whatever lives down there, and clues in the rooms reveal which. The squad needs a key card from each floor and everyone back in the elevator before the doors shut.

**The gap.** The Escape genre has the strongest lift in this archetype (lift 3.92, 29% of hits vs 18% of other games), but it is moderate evidence. Entrants are mostly creature-chase games. A rule-deduction escape with random rule combinations fills that gap and keeps replay value, since leaders' hits depend on repeatable rounds (79% rounds).

**Core loop.** The elevator arrives on a floor and shows the five rules. The squad leaves in pairs, searches rooms for a key card and clues, and listens for monster behaviour that reveals the false rule. They must obey the true rules and break the lie to win safely. Breaking a true rule triggers a hazard. When the key card is placed in the elevator reader the doors close, and the squad has 90 seconds to get everyone aboard. Deeper floors mix new rule sets, and a wipe ends the run.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-8s: the elevator doors open on Floor 1 and the rule board appears. 8-25s: an easy rule ('don't whistle') is demonstrated by a scripted victim. 25-45s: players find the janitor's note that exposes the false rule. 45-60s: key card collected, doors chime, and the elevator descends with the Floor 1 trophy.

**Features**
- Procedural rule boards from a library of about 40 rules, giving thousands of combinations (judgement)
- Clue system per floor: janitor notes, scorch marks, a flickering monitor (judgement)
- Escape-style objective with a key card per floor and an elevator departure timer (measured: Escape genre lift 3.92)
- Runs last about 14 minutes and end on a win or a wipe (measured: rounds, 79% of hits)
- Wipe sends the squad back to the lobby, keeping only cosmetics and floor-record stats (measured: can_lose_progress lift 2.05)
- Four-player squads, no revive after the second floor, so each death matters (judgement)
- Floor Record leaderboards per friend group (judgement)
- Face-in-the-doorway icon, with a small text label for the rule board (measured: 71% of hits show a face)
- Descriptive title and no codes in the description (measured: 100% of hits)
- Hazard design that is readable on a small phone screen (judgement)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: First floor cleared and a Floor 1 elevator sticker.
- First session: Reach Floor 4 and unlock the 'Safety Inspector' badge.
- First week: Clear the basement once and unlock a brass-elevator cosmetic.
- Progression: Cosmetic-only: elevator skins, uniform pieces and notice-board stickers, plus a personal deepest-floor record.
- Retention hook: Day 2: a daily rulebook floor with a fixed rule set the whole server competes on.
- Retention hook: Day 8: a new building theme (hospital wing) with new rule categories.

**Social mechanic.** Players must argue and vote which rule is the lie. Pairs split up to search and radio back clues with a quick-chat wheel.

**Monetization.** Brass Elevator Skin (99 R$: Cosmetic elevator look), Inspector Uniform (149 R$: Cosmetic outfit with a lantern trail), Rulebook Archive (199 R$: Access to a cosmetic gallery of every rule board seen, no gameplay advantage). First prompt at minute 11.0. Pass min price of 99 and median 149 match hits' medians. No VIP, since this is a four-player squad game and 50% of hits do not have VIP. No boost: 50% of hits skip it and rule clarity must stay fair.

**Content cadence.** Weekly new rule cards, a new building every 3 weeks, and a limited seasonal-themed floor monthly.

**Versus the leaders.** DOORS is a linear room-by-room chase and Sarab and Dandy's World are about reacting to a creature. Floor Rules is about deduction and discussion, with a different challenge every ride. It differs from the already-proposed Split Sight Horror Escape, where each player sees different things, because here everyone sees the same board and has to infer the lie.

**Declared trait profile** (what the scoring agent checks): core_loop=horror, play_mode=['coop', 'pve'], session_shape=rounds, progression_type=['cosmetic_only'], social_asymmetry=none, can_lose_progress=True, has_base_defense=False, offline_accrual=False, has_timers_or_fomo=False, borrows_meme_or_ip=none, title_pattern=descriptive, icon_has_face=True, icon_has_text=True, desc_has_codes=False, pass_has_boost=False, pass_has_vip=False, server_size=16, pass_count=3, pass_min_price=99, pass_median_price=149, ttfr_target_seconds=45, first_purchase_prompt_min=11.0, round_length_s=840

**Scope.** ~5 weeks · 1 scripter and 1 environment artist · biggest risk: Rule-and-clue pairings must be solvable but not trivial. If players learn the rule library the deduction is over, so a daily-cycled rule pool is needed.

**Kill early if.** In week 1, more than 50% of runs end on Floor 1 or 2, or more than 80% of squads identify the lie in under 30 seconds.

### #21 · Whiteout Station: Keep the Stove Lit  —  horror / Co-op PvE
*A blizzard hides everything past a few steps and something follows the cold, so your team keeps a stove alive for one night and each flare you fire lights the way home but also shows you to the creature.*

**Pitch.** A crew of up to 12 is stranded at a mountain research station in a whiteout. The stove keeps everyone warm but burns through fuel, so someone must go into the storm to fetch more from outposts and supply crates. A creature in the snow stalks anyone who strays from the glow. Each flare reveals the map for 20 seconds, but also marks you to the thing, so the team decides when it is worth it.

**The gap.** 99 Nights in the Forest (peak 1.9M) proved that co-op survival horror at scale works, but it is a long multi-day campaign. Hits here favour rounds (79%) and the data shows few entrants with a base to defend (only 21% of hits have base defense, lift 1.35, moderate evidence). A short 14-minute round with a base-and-expedition loop fits mobile sessions. Growth is 9.2% over 7 days at median, so the archetype is still rising.

**Core loop.** Each round is one night of 14 minutes. Players stay near the stove to keep warm and craft, or go out in pairs, tied to the station by landmarks, to collect fuel and parts from outposts. Visibility in the storm is about 8 studs, so players find the way back by the smoke column and the shared flare map. The creature drifts toward whoever is far from the stove or has fired a flare. If the stove goes out, warmth drains and everyone must reach the radio tower at the far end. Survive until dawn and the helicopter extracts the team.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-10s: spawn in the warm station with a scripted storm outside. 10-25s: pick up the fuel can and walk out the door into the whiteout. 25-45s: find a supply crate by following a lantern line. 45-60s: carry fuel back, watch the stove flare, and earn the first warmth-token reward.

**Features**
- 14-minute night rounds with extraction at dawn (measured: 79% of hits are rounds)
- Stove as a central base: fuel burns down, and teammates feed it (measured: has_base_defense lift 1.35, moderate)
- Whiteout fog with 8-stud visibility, and the smoke column as a landmark (judgement)
- Shared flare map reveal for 20 seconds that also alerts the creature (judgement)
- Lose carried supplies if you freeze, but keep unlocked cosmetics (measured: can_lose_progress lift 2.05)
- Revive-by-warming a downed teammate at the stove (measured: 50% of hits use help asymmetry)
- Two station maps at launch: ridge hut and mine head (judgement)
- Hood-and-scarf face on the icon with frost around it (measured: 71% of hits have a face)
- Logbook wall showing best night times per squad, no numeric stat progression (judgement)
- Server of 12 with all players in one station, instead of the 25 median, so the stove is crowded but not chaotic (judgement)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: Return fuel to the stove and see the station light up.
- First session: Survive a first night and get a dawn-extraction cutscene and a logbook entry.
- First week: Survive 5 nights and unlock a winter-gear cosmetic set.
- Progression: No stat progression. Rewards are cosmetic gear, lantern colours and logbook records for fastest dawn and most fuel hauled.
- Retention hook: Day 2: a new storm variant (longer night, thicker fog) in the rotation.
- Retention hook: Day 8: a new station map with a different creature behaviour.

**Social mechanic.** Players choose between staying at the stove or exploring. They revive each other by warming and call for flares with a quick-chat wheel.

**Monetization.** Frost Gear Set (99 R$: Cosmetic outfit and boots), Lantern Colors (149 R$: Cosmetic lantern and flare colours), Private Station (199 R$: VIP private server for friend groups). First prompt at minute 15.0. Prompt after the first full night ends (about 14 minutes plus the lobby), so purchases never interrupt a scare. Price points match hits' medians (min 99, median 149). VIP is included because half of hits have it. No boost (hit passes without boosts are 50%).

**Content cadence.** New storm variants every 2 weeks, a new map monthly, and a seasonal winter event.

**Versus the leaders.** 99 Nights in the Forest is a multi-night, long-run survival game with a big map and crafting. Whiteout Station is one short night around a single stove, with fog and a flare tradeoff. It is not Deep Salvage's shared air hose or Quiet Hours' noise meter, because the central resource is a base fire and the tradeoff is visibility versus being hunted.

**Declared trait profile** (what the scoring agent checks): core_loop=horror, play_mode=['coop', 'pve'], session_shape=rounds, progression_type=['none'], social_asymmetry=help, can_lose_progress=True, has_base_defense=True, offline_accrual=False, has_timers_or_fomo=False, borrows_meme_or_ip=none, title_pattern=descriptive, icon_has_face=True, icon_has_text=False, desc_has_codes=False, pass_has_boost=False, pass_has_vip=True, server_size=12, pass_count=3, pass_min_price=99, pass_median_price=149, ttfr_target_seconds=40, first_purchase_prompt_min=15.0, round_length_s=840

**Scope.** ~5 weeks · 1 scripter, 1 builder and 1 VFX/sound designer · biggest risk: Fog and snow effects can run badly on low-end phones, and visibility that is too low may feel frustrating. Test early with an 8-stud and a 14-stud setting.

**Kill early if.** In week 1, more than 60% of rounds end with the stove going out in the first 6 minutes, or median playtime per session is under 8 minutes.

### #22 · Pop a Prize Balloon  —  rng / PvE
*Throw darts at floating balloons, and every pop drops a random plush with a face for your carnival shelf.*

**Pitch.** You run a tiny carnival booth. Colored balloons drift up, and you pop them with darts to reveal plush toys of different rarities. Each plush you put on your shelf earns tickets every second, and tickets buy a bigger booth, faster darts and rarer balloon batches. It is an RNG-plus-tycoon game with a simple aiming action, so it feels more physical than a plain roll button.

**The gap.** All 6 hits combine numbers_go_up with rarity_collection (100% vs 47% of other games, lift 1.80). All 6 hit icons have a face (100% vs 53%). Tycoon is the genre of 67% of hits vs 40% of others (lift 1.95). The leaders (Kick a Lucky Block, Launch a Lucky Block, Throw a Coin) all use a physical verb on one object. Nobody pairs a physical verb with a collectible shelf that earns income. Median hit rate is 29% on 21 games and the 7-day trend is -12%, so we need a clear angle, not another clone.

**Core loop.** Tap or hold to aim and throw a dart at a balloon. The pop reveals a plush with a rarity, and the plush goes onto a shelf slot. Each shelf plush earns tickets per second. Spend tickets on more shelf slots, a longer dart range, faster throws, and higher-tier balloon batches whose odds shift toward rare plush. Rare plush have mutations such as 'Glitter' or 'Sleepy' that multiply income, so duplicates stay interesting. A shelf-full prompt pushes the player to sell the weakest plush for tickets, which keeps the loop moving.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-5s: spawn at the booth with a dart in hand and one glowing balloon. 5-10s: the first pop is a guaranteed uncommon plush that jumps onto the shelf and tickets start ticking. 10-30s: three more balloons rise, and a rare-colored one appears so the player sees what the game is about. 30-45s: the first upgrade, a faster dart, is affordable and flashes. 45-60s: the booth grows a second shelf row and a bigger balloon batch floats in with a fanfare.

**Features**
- Aim-and-throw dart popping with generous auto-aim for phones (judgement)
- Balloon colors show the tier before the pop, so there is anticipation (judgement)
- Plush shelf slots that earn tickets per second (measured: tycoon + numbers_go_up hits)
- Rarity tiers with 'Glitter', 'Sleepy' and similar mutation modifiers (measured: rarity_collection hits)
- Plush all have big faces, and the icon shows the best plush's face (measured: icon_has_face 100% of hits)
- Collection book with completion bonuses per plush family (judgement)
- Auto-dart upgrade bought with tickets, so there is always a reason to stay (judgement)
- A progress bar toward the next booth upgrade, always visible (judgement)
- Hourly 'Golden Balloon' timed event (judgement; timers are about 50% of hits and neutral)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: A guaranteed plush on the shelf and the first ticket income.
- First session: 3-4 booth upgrades, a rare plush with a mutation, and the first collection-book page complete.
- First week: A second booth theme unlocked, with a shelf of 10+ plush with mutations.
- Progression: The booth grows from a cart to a tent to a big-top, and each stage unlocks a new balloon tier and plush family.
- Retention hook: Day 2: a free daily 'Golden Balloon' guarantees a rare-or-better plush.
- Retention hook: Day 8: the first plush family (set of 8) completes for a permanent income bonus and a unique balloon skin.

**Social mechanic.** Everyone on the server (7 players) sees each other's rare pops as a confetti burst and a name banner. A shared 'server crowd' meter grants everyone a small luck bonus as it fills. Nobody can take from or help anybody else.

**Monetization.** Lucky Darts (299 R$: +50% rare balloon odds, permanent), Auto Dart (199 R$: Throws darts automatically at the nearest balloon), Double Tickets (449 R$: x2 ticket income), VIP Booth (699 R$: Golden booth skin, VIP chat tag, exclusive plush each month), Extra Shelf Row (149 R$: Two extra shelf slots from the start). First prompt at minute 4.0. The price shape follows the hit medians: min 149, median about 300, 5 passes (hits' median is 5). The first prompt comes after the player has felt the auto-throw's value, not in the first minute. Boost passes show up in 67% of hits. Prices and the prompt minute are design judgement.

**Content cadence.** A weekly new plush family (8 plush) and a new balloon type, a monthly new booth theme, and a Golden Balloon hour each day.

**Versus the leaders.** Kick a Lucky Block, Launch a Lucky Block and Throw a Coin all reward a hit with a one-off drop. Here the drop becomes a plush on a shelf that earns income, so the collection itself is the tycoon. The theme (carnival and plush) is not seen among the leaders, and the plush faces fit the icon trait that all hits share.

**Declared trait profile** (what the scoring agent checks): core_loop=rng, play_mode=['pve'], session_shape=persistent, progression_type=['numbers_go_up', 'rarity_collection'], social_asymmetry=none, can_lose_progress=False, has_base_defense=False, offline_accrual=False, has_timers_or_fomo=True, borrows_meme_or_ip=none, title_pattern=verb_a_noun, icon_has_face=True, icon_has_text=True, desc_has_codes=False, pass_has_boost=True, pass_has_vip=True, server_size=7, pass_count=5, pass_min_price=149, pass_median_price=299, ttfr_target_seconds=10, first_purchase_prompt_min=4.0

**Scope.** ~3 weeks · 1 dev, 1 part-time artist · biggest risk: Dart aiming on mobile may feel fiddly, so the auto-aim assist must be tuned well.

**Kill early if.** Fewer than 1 in 4 new players reach the second shelf row, or median session length is under 5 minutes by day 3 of the test (design judgement thresholds).

### #23 · Crack a Geode  —  rng / PvE
*Smash plain rocks to find a random crystal inside, then leave it in your tumbler to polish into something worth far more.*

**Pitch.** Geodes tumble out of a cave wall and you crack them with a hammer. Each one holds a crystal of random rarity and shape, and some are mutated. Put crystals in the tumbler and it keeps polishing them while you are away, so you come back to a bigger payout. A smiling rock mascot named Pebble guides you and appears on the icon.

**The gap.** Offline accrual is carried by 33% of hits vs 53% of other games, but its global lift is 2.01 (weak evidence: fewer than 3 hits). Few RNG leaders have a reason to come back after leaving. The 2-day median half-life is from a window of only 13 days, but it suggests that games with no return hook decay fast. Median staying power is 0.60 (n=10). The tumbler gives a day-2 return reason that the instant-reward leaders lack.

**Core loop.** Swing the hammer at a geode to crack it. It reveals a crystal with a rarity and a mutation, such as 'Frosted' or 'Twin'. Rough crystals sell for a few coins, but placing them in the tumbler raises their value over real time. Cleaner crystals pay far more when polished. Coins buy a stronger hammer, more tumbler slots and deeper cave levels with better geode odds. Polished crystals also go into a display case that gives a permanent income bonus per set.

**First 60 seconds.** 0-5s: spawn at the cave mouth, Pebble waves, one geode is already glowing. 5-12s: the first crack is guaranteed and shows a nice crystal with a satisfying shatter. 12-25s: Pebble points at the tumbler and the player drops the crystal in, and a timer starts. 25-45s: three more geodes roll down and the player cracks them by tapping. 45-60s: the first hammer upgrade is affordable, and a 'your tumbler is polishing' notice shows what waits when they return.

**Features**
- Hammer crack with a short shatter animation and rarity-colored light burst (judgement)
- Tumbler with 20 min to 8 h polish timers that continue offline (measured: offline accrual lift 2.01, weak)
- Rarity tiers plus mutation modifiers (measured: rarity_collection progression in all hits)
- Pebble the mascot with a face on the icon and as a guide (measured: icon_has_face 100% of hits)
- Deeper cave levels that unlock better odds (judgement)
- Display case with set bonuses for completed crystal families (judgement)
- Welcome-back summary screen showing polished crystals and coins earned (judgement)
- Short daily 'Geode Rain' event that doubles geode drops for 15 minutes (judgement; timers are 50% of hits)

**Rewards and retention**
- First minute: A guaranteed rare crystal and the first tumbler start.
- First session: 2-3 cave levels, a hammer upgrade, and the first polished crystal collected.
- First week: A display case with 2 full sets and 6+ tumbler slots.
- Progression: Hammers (wood to diamond), cave depths and tumbler slots raise income and rarity odds. Collections give permanent bonuses.
- Retention hook: Day 2: the tumbler finished while away, with a bonus for any crystal left overnight.
- Retention hook: Day 8: a seven-day login chain ends in an exclusive 'Moonstone' crystal and Pebble costume.

**Social mechanic.** Each player's tumbler is visible to everyone on the server, and a shared 'cave lantern' brightens as players crack geodes. At certain levels the whole server gets a small luck boost. Players cannot take from or help each other.

**Monetization.** Lucky Hammer (349 R$: +40% chance of rarer crystals), Extra Tumblers (199 R$: Four more tumbler slots), Fast Polish (449 R$: Polishing finishes twice as fast), Auto Crack (299 R$: Cracks nearby geodes automatically), Crystal Club VIP (749 R$: Gold hammer skin, VIP tag, one exclusive crystal a month). First prompt at minute 5.0. The prompt comes after the player has waited on the first tumbler timer, when Fast Polish makes sense. Medians follow the hit numbers, and the minute is a judgement.

**Content cadence.** A new cave level every 2 weeks, a new crystal family weekly, and monthly seasonal mutations.

**Versus the leaders.** Leaders such as Kick a Lucky Block and Anime Dice reward the roll instantly and have no reason to return. Crack a Geode adds a polish timer that continues offline, with the mine theme and a rock mascot. It is different from the already-proposed Dig a Dinosaur because there are no skeletons or museum: it is a quick crack, then wait-and-polish.

**Scope.** ~3 weeks · 1 dev, 1 part-time artist · biggest risk: Offline accrual can reduce the time players spend in-game, and the 13-day history makes the retention effect of timers uncertain.
